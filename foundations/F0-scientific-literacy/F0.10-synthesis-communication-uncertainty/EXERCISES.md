# F0.10 — Independent assessment

**Module:** Synthesis and communication of uncertainty  
**Total:** 100 points  
**Local pass gate:** `>=80/100 + no critical fail`  
**Rule:** complete without `ANSWER_KEY.md`.

This assessment tests whether you can preserve the same epistemic content while changing audience and language.

A response that is rhetorically polished but materially changes magnitude, uncertainty, certainty, applicability or inferential scope loses credit.

---

# Section A — Calibrated language and claim scope — 20 points

## A1. Repair the binary claim — 5 points

A trial reports:

`MD = -9 s [95% CI -41,+23]`, `p=0.41`.

Outcome is time, so lower is better. A colleague writes:

> “The intervention does not work because p>0.05.”

Rewrite this as one scientifically defensible sentence and explain which information the original sentence discarded.

## A2. High certainty is not large effect — 5 points

A body of evidence has:

- pooled effect `+0.18 unit`;
- 95% CI `+0.14 to +0.22`;
- practical-benefit threshold `+1.0`;
- high certainty.

Explain why both statements below are wrong and replace each:

1. “High certainty means the intervention has a large important effect.”
2. “Because the effect is trivial, certainty must be low.”

## A3. Directness versus bias — 5 points

A study exactly matches the target population/intervention/comparator/outcome/time, but has differential attrition and unblinded subjective outcome assessment.

Explain why `direct evidence` does not imply `unbiased evidence`.

## A4. Evidence statement versus recommendation — 5 points

Write:

1. one evidence statement for a body showing a small benefit with moderate certainty;
2. one sentence explaining why this alone does not determine whether a recommendation should be strong.

# Section B — Estimate + interval + threshold — 20 points

For B1–B4, outcome is race time; lower is better. The predefined threshold for an important benefit is `-30 s`.

## B1 — 5 points

`MD = -12 s [95% CI -44,+20]`

Classify what the data are compatible with relative to:

- the null;
- the `-30 s` threshold;
- possible harm/worsening.

Then write one calibrated sentence.

## B2 — 5 points

`MD = -4 s [95% CI -18,+10]`

Is it defensible to say the data are compatible with **no important benefit of at least 30 s**? Explain precisely. Does that prove exact zero effect?

## B3 — 5 points

A superiority trial reports `p=0.62` and no equivalence margin.

Explain why “the treatments are equivalent” is not established.

## B4 — 5 points

Compare:

- Study 1: `MD = -25 s [-90,+40]`;
- Study 2: `MD = -7 s [-15,+1]`.

Which study better constrains effects relative to the `-30 s` threshold, and why? Do not use the word “significant” as your main reasoning.

# Section C — Conflict, false balance and uncertainty source — 20 points

## C1. False-balance audit — 8 points

A social-media summary says:

> “The literature is split: two sources support Intervention X and two do not, so the evidence is 50/50.”

The four sources are:

1. a direct low-risk randomized trial with a narrow CI around a small effect;
2. a systematic review of direct randomized trials with a similar small pooled effect;
3. an observational cohort with a much larger favorable association but strong residual-confounding concerns;
4. an acute mechanistic experiment showing a biomarker response but no chronic performance outcome.

Explain why `2 versus 2` is invalid synthesis. State at least four properties that should determine rhetorical/evidential weight.

## C2. Name the uncertainty mechanism — 8 points

For each statement, identify the dominant uncertainty source as `bias`, `imprecision`, `inconsistency`, `indirectness`, or `missing/dissemination evidence` and justify briefly.

1. CI crosses both a trivial-effect region and a large-benefit threshold.
2. Five direct studies give materially different effects without a credible explanation.
3. All studies are in sedentary older adults but the target is elite endurance athletes.
4. Differential missingness may depend on true outcome values.

## C3. Update conditions — 4 points

A synthesis currently concludes “a large benefit is not well supported, but a small benefit remains plausible.”

Give two concrete future findings that could materially strengthen or weaken that conclusion. “More research” alone earns no credit.

# Section D — Fixed evidence package: preserve epistemic content across audiences — 40 points

Use the **same package below** for every answer.

## D0. Target question

For **trained adult endurance athletes**, does **Intervention X**, used daily during **8–12 weeks** of training, improve **20-km time-trial performance** compared with matched placebo/no Intervention X?

Outcome = seconds to completion; lower is better.

**Practical-benefit threshold:** `-30 s`.

This is a synthetic educational package. Intervention X is intentionally unspecified and must not be converted into a real nutrition recommendation.

## D0.1 Evidence source A — systematic review/meta-analysis

- 6 randomized trials;
- total `n=310`;
- mostly trained cyclists/runners;
- broadly comparable Intervention X versus placebo/control;
- pooled random-effects `MD = -20 s [95% CI -42,+2]`;
- `I² = 28%`;
- four trials low RoB, two some concerns;
- no strong missing-evidence signal identified, but the number of studies is small;
- review methods judged adequate.

## D0.2 Evidence source B — newer direct RCT

- preregistered before enrollment;
- `n=220` trained cyclists;
- 12 weeks;
- matched placebo;
- objective electronically timed performance outcome;
- low attrition;
- low risk of bias for this result;
- `MD = -12 s [95% CI -27,+3]`.

## D0.3 Evidence source C — observational cohort

- `n=1,400` recreational runners;
- Intervention X self-selected;
- adjusted association with 10-km race time: `-48 s [95% CI -62,-34]`;
- baseline training volume/diet measured imperfectly;
- residual confounding is plausible;
- population and outcome differ from target.

## D0.4 Evidence source D — acute mechanistic crossover

- `n=18` trained cyclists;
- one acute exercise session;
- biomarker increases by `30% [95% CI 18%,42%]` with Intervention X;
- no chronic performance outcome measured.

## D0.5 Evidence source E — small newer RCT

- `n=32` trained runners;
- 8 weeks;
- direct performance outcome;
- `MD = -75 s [95% CI -140,-10]`;
- 25% attrition, imbalanced by group;
- multiple eligible analysis versions, with only one highlighted;
- high risk of bias for the highlighted performance result.

## D0.6 Body-level appraisal supplied for this communication task

An independent methods team integrating the **direct randomized performance evidence** judged the certainty for the target outcome **MODERATE**.

Main reason confidence is not high: the combined evidence still leaves material uncertainty around the `-30 s` practical-benefit threshold, with the large favorable estimate coming from a small high-risk result.

The observational and mechanistic sources are considered **supporting/contextual**, not co-equal estimators of the target chronic performance effect.

No formal recommendation has been made because benefits/harms, costs, feasibility, preferences and other decision criteria were not evaluated.

## D1. Invariant core — 10 points

Before writing prose, extract the invariant core:

1. target;
2. direction;
3. magnitude pattern;
4. precision;
5. threshold relationship;
6. certainty;
7. applicability;
8. inferential boundaries/update conditions.

Your answer must explicitly explain why the large favorable observational/mechanistic signals do not receive equal weight to the direct randomized performance evidence.

## D2. Technical synthesis — 10 points

Write a technical evidence synthesis of **120–170 words**.

It must preserve:

- target population/context;
- strongest direct estimates;
- CI/threshold relation;
- moderate certainty;
- why the discordant large effect is down-weighted;
- mechanistic-versus-performance distinction;
- evidence statement versus recommendation boundary;
- what remains uncertain.

## D3. Practitioner-facing synthesis — 8 points

Write **80–120 words** for a sports professional who understands basic research concepts but does not need statistical jargon.

It must remain no more certain than D2.

## D4. Lay-facing synthesis — 8 points

Write **60–100 words** for a non-specialist.

You may translate confidence intervals into ordinary language, but you may not delete the fact that meaningful benefit and little/no benefit remain plausible across the strongest body.

Do not turn the evidence statement into advice.

## D5. Language-drift audit — 4 points

Compare D2–D4. Identify at least four facts/qualifiers that must be identical in substance across all three.

Then state whether any version accidentally became more certain, more causal, broader in population, or more recommendation-like than D2. Repair it if necessary.

# Critical fails

Any one of the following blocks a pass regardless of total score:

1. `p<0.05 = true/important`;
2. `p>0.05 = no effect/equivalence`;
3. materially important CI/threshold information is hidden or discarded to simplify the message;
4. low certainty is translated as “no effect”;
5. high certainty is translated as “large/important effect”;
6. direct evidence is treated as automatically unbiased;
7. weak and strong sources receive equal rhetorical weight solely for “balance”;
8. body certainty is collapsed into recommendation strength;
9. population/outcome/time/context is generalized beyond support without justification;
10. causal language is strengthened beyond the evidence design;
11. practitioner/lay wording becomes more certain than the technical synthesis;
12. the observational association or acute biomarker result is presented as direct proof of the target chronic performance effect;
13. the conclusion is based on vote-counting studies;
14. a recommendation is issued from D0 despite the explicit absence of a recommendation assessment.
