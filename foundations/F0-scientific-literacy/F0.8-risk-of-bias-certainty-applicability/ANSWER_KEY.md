# Critical-fail review

Before assigning the numeric score, check whether the response endorsed any critical-fail statement from `EXERCISES.md`.

# Section A — Conceptual separation — 25 points

## A1. Five objects — 5 points

1. Search strategy/exclusions described → **reporting completeness**.
2. Missing outcomes systematically shift result → **risk of bias**.
3. Confidence true effect is above +1.0 → **certainty of evidence**.
4. Recreational adults → elite endurance athletes → **applicability/transportability**.
5. Strong versus conditional guideline action → **recommendation strength**.

`1 point each`.

Critical distinction: `critical appraisal` is the broader process that may include several of these judgments; the prompt asks for the primary object.

## A2. Reporting fallacy — 4 points

Full-credit repair:

> CONSORT/PRISMA completion improves visibility of what was planned, done and reported. It does not establish that the design, conduct, analysis or result is unbiased, nor that the body of evidence has high certainty. Separate risk-of-bias and certainty appraisal is required.

Scoring:

- identifies reporting purpose in each case: 2;
- explicitly rejects low-RoB/high-certainty inference: 1;
- names separate appraisal need: 1.

## A3. Bias versus imprecision — 4 points

- Study A is more precise because its CI is much narrower.
- A's supplied problem is systematic measurement bias.
- B's supplied weakness is imprecision/random uncertainty.
- Narrow uncertainty around A does not remove systematic displacement from unblinded measurement.

`1 point each`.

## A4. Result-specific RoB — 4 points

Expected reasoning:

- automated time-trial may have lower measurement-bias concern;
- self-reported recovery collected under unblinded conditions may have different measurement/deviation vulnerabilities;
- blinded biomarker has another measurement structure;
- missingness/selection of analysis may also differ by outcome/result.

The correct object is a **specific result/effect estimate**, not an undifferentiated study score.

## A5. Certainty versus magnitude — 4 points

1. Invalid — high certainty can concern trivial/null-range effects.
2. Valid.
3. Invalid — low certainty means low confidence about effect range/threshold, not zero effect.
4. Valid.

`1 point each`, but invalid items require repair for credit.

## A6. Certainty versus recommendation — 4 points

Full-credit answer mentions at least four of:

- multiple critical outcomes;
- desirable/undesirable effects;
- certainty across outcomes/net effect;
- values/preferences;
- resources/cost;
- equity;
- acceptability;
- feasibility;
- context.

The conclusion must state that certainty for one outcome is an input, not recommendation strength itself.

# Section B — RoB 2 and ROBINS-I — 25 points

## B1. RoB 2 domains — 5 points

1. bias arising from the randomization process;
2. bias due to deviations from intended interventions;
3. bias due to missing outcome data;
4. bias in measurement of the outcome;
5. bias in selection of the reported result.

`1 point each`.

No additional points for converting them into a total score; doing so as the final method is a critical fail.

## B2. RCT audit — 10 points

A strong answer:

### Randomization process

**Low concern from supplied facts.**

Computerized sequence + adequate concealment support the randomized contrast. No supplied evidence of manipulation.

### Deviations from intended interventions

**Insufficient information / bounded concern.**

Symptoms/training tolerance may involve intervention consequences, but exact RoB judgment depends on the effect of interest and handling of deviations. Do not infer automatically from missingness alone.

### Missing outcome data

**High/material concern.**

22% versus 6%, with missingness following symptoms and poor tolerance, makes dependence on true outcome plausible; completers-only analysis can distort the randomized comparison.

### Measurement of outcome

**Low concern from supplied facts.**

Automated timing limits subjective differential outcome assessment, assuming the protocol itself is comparable.

### Selection of reported result

**Low concern from supplied facts, with normal residual uncertainty.**

Registry predates enrollment and matches primary outcome/timepoint; no supplied evidence of multiple alternative primary analyses.

### Overall bounded interpretation

Example:

> The primary time-trial result benefits from a credible randomization process and objective measurement, but differential, outcome-related missingness plus completers-only analysis can materially bias the result. Therefore the randomized label does not justify treating this specific estimate as automatically low risk.

Scoring guide:

- five domain discussions: 1.5 each = 7.5;
- overall result-specific conclusion: 2.5.

Exact official RoB 2 judgment is not required because the vignette intentionally omits some signalling-question detail.

## B3. Why randomization is not enough — 3 points

Any three valid post-randomization mechanisms, e.g.:

- deviations/nonadherence/co-interventions;
- missing outcomes/attrition;
- differential outcome measurement;
- selective result analysis/reporting;
- inappropriate post-randomization selection.

`1 point each`.

## B4. ROBINS-I target-trial logic — 4 points

Expected:

- specify the hypothetical randomized trial that would answer the same intervention-effect question;
- compare eligibility, intervention classification, time zero/follow-up, confounding control, outcome, analysis/effect of interest;
- use divergence from that benchmark to reason about bias;
- observational data remain observational because no actual random assignment is created and unmeasured confounding/data limitations may remain.

## B5. Version literacy — 3 points

Full credit:

- revised ROBINS-I V2 draft posted **20 November 2025**;
- explicitly **still draft / subject to change**;
- one current structural feature, e.g. algorithms for domain judgments, triage to Critical risk, six-domain structure, or removal of the separate deviations-from-intended-intervention domain from the Nov-2025 draft.

# Section C — GRADE certainty and applicability — 25 points

## C1. Four certainty categories — 4 points

- High;
- Moderate;
- Low;
- Very low.

For full credit, learner must connect certainty to confidence about where the true effect lies relative to a decision-relevant range/threshold for an outcome/question/body of evidence.

## C2. Five downgrading domains — 5 points

1. risk of bias;
2. inconsistency;
3. indirectness;
4. imprecision;
5. dissemination/publication/non-reporting bias.

`1 point each`.

## C3. Threshold-aware imprecision — 5 points

Threshold = +1.0.

### Body A

`+1.5 [1.2, 1.8]`

- entire CI lies above +1.0;
- under the supplied threshold, values remain in the meaningful-benefit region;
- comparatively precise for this decision.

### Body B

`+1.5 [-0.4, 3.4]`

- includes possible harm/negative effect;
- includes null/trivial effects;
- includes meaningful and large benefit;
- therefore does not resolve the decision-relevant category well.

Scoring:

- A interpretation: 2;
- B zones: 2;
- explicit comparison without significance shortcut: 1.

## C4. Inconsistency without I² worship — 4 points

### Body C

Low I² only describes limited detected statistical heterogeneity in the included studies. It says nothing by itself about indirectness from narrow population/context, RoB, imprecision or missing evidence.

### Body D

High I² does not automatically invalidate pooling or require a downgrade. If estimates remain in the same decision range and a plausible **prespecified** explanation accounts for variation, inconsistency may be less decision-threatening. Context, effect pattern and uncertainty matter.

`2 points each`.

## C5. Indirectness/applicability matrix — 5 points

Expected mismatches:

- **Population:** recreational vs trained endurance; plausible effect modification by training status/baseline performance.
- **Intervention:** half dose vs target dose; dose-response or tolerability may alter effect.
- **Comparator:** placebo vs active control; contrast itself differs.
- **Outcome:** physiological surrogate vs field performance; surrogate may not map to target outcome.
- **Time:** 2 weeks vs 12 weeks; adaptation/effect durability may differ.
- **Setting:** temperate indoor vs hot outdoor; environment may modify physiology/performance.

Full credit requires mechanism-based reasoning, not simply counting six mismatches. Award up to 5 based on coverage/quality.

## C6. Directness versus bias — 2 points

Repair:

> Exact target-population match improves directness/applicability but does not establish internal validity. Confounding, selection, missingness, measurement or selective-reporting mechanisms can still bias the result.

# Section D — Integrated audit — 25 points

A high-quality answer should resemble the structure below.

## 1. Target question/outcome

Trained adult endurance athletes; Intervention X vs control; synthetic performance score; ~12 weeks; higher better; practical threshold +1.0.

## 2. Reporting visibility

A provides several visibility signals: registrations and search procedures are described. B's summary supplies no comparable prespecification/search detail. Absence from the vignette should be described as unknown/not demonstrated, not automatically misconduct.

## 3. Design/effect of interest

A: randomized intervention-effect body.

B: non-randomized self-selected exposure/intervention body aiming at an intervention-effect interpretation, requiring strong confounding/selection assumptions.

## 4. Result-level RoB mechanisms

A: supplied facts suggest protected randomization, objective measurement, low missingness, preregistration alignment.

B: baseline motivation/training confounding, crude confounder measurement/residual confounding, differential dropout/completers-only selection, subjective proxy measurement, possible analysis flexibility.

## 5. Comparative RoB judgment

A is lower concern from the supplied data.

B has materially greater result-level bias concerns that can plausibly move estimates across the practical threshold.

Do not say “B unusable because observational”.

## 6. Estimate + threshold

A: `+1.5 [1.2,1.8]`; CI fully above +1.0.

B: `+1.6 [-0.2,3.4]`; point estimate similar but CI includes negative/trivial and meaningful/large positive effects.

## 7. Inconsistency

A effects `+1.2 to +1.7` remain in a narrow decision-relevant range.

B effects `-0.3 to +3.8` span materially different conclusions and larger effects cluster in shorter studies, requiring explanation rather than automatic moderator story.

## 8. Indirectness

A: close population/intervention/duration/outcome match.

B: recreational/untrained populations, 2–4 week follow-up, subjective proxies in several studies, all less direct to trained adults at ~12 weeks.

## 9. Imprecision

A substantially more precise relative to +1.0 threshold.

B leaves several decision categories compatible.

## 10. Missing/dissemination evidence

A has registry/unpublished-result search efforts, which improve visibility but do not prove no missing evidence.

B: unresolved from supplied summary; do not infer absence or presence without evidence.

## 11. Overall certainty

A should be judged materially higher certainty than B based on the supplied information.

A could plausibly support relatively high certainty if no hidden material concern exists.

B would be lower because multiple bias concerns, inconsistency, indirectness and imprecision remain.

The exact final GRADE category is less important than transparent domain reasoning in this synthetic task.

## 12. Applicability

A: relatively direct to target.

B: limited transfer to trained adults at 12 weeks.

This is a separate judgment from internal bias/certainty.

## 13. Recommendation-strength boundary

The evidence assessment does not include all decision criteria. A final recommendation may require harms, other outcomes, values, resources, feasibility, acceptability, equity and net-effect reasoning.

### Required explicit statements

Full-credit answer contains all:

- similar point estimates do not imply similar certainty;
- observational design alone does not make B unusable;
- randomization alone does not exempt A from RoB appraisal;
- B's CI spans materially different decision ranges;
- certainty and applicability are separate;
- certainty of one performance outcome is not recommendation strength.

### Scoring

- fields 1–5: 8 points;
- fields 6–10: 9 points;
- fields 11–13: 5 points;
- all required explicit statements / epistemic calibration: 3 points.

# Error-routing guide

Observed error → likely prerequisite/remediation:

- `RCT = low risk automatically` → F0.3 + F0.8 RoB 2;
- `observational = useless` → F0.2/F0.3 target-trial reasoning + F0.8 ROBINS-I;
- narrow CI treated as unbiased → F0.3 random vs systematic error + F0.5 precision;
- low I² treated as certainty → F0.7 heterogeneity + F0.8 inconsistency;
- p-value treated as certainty → F0.5;
- directness treated as internal validity → F0.3 external validity + F0.8 indirectness;
- low certainty interpreted as no effect → F0.5 estimate/CI + F0.8 certainty;
- one-outcome certainty treated as recommendation → F0.8 recommendation boundary.
