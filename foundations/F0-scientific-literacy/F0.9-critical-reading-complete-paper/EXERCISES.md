# F0.9 — Exercícios e avaliação independente

**Assessment state:** `READY`

**Total:** 100 points

**Local pass gate:** `>=80/100 + no critical fail`

**Important:** complete the real-paper appraisal before opening `ANSWER_KEY.md`.

This assessment tests integration of F0.1–F0.8. It is not an article-summary exercise.

---

# General rules

1. For the performance paper, use the **full article**, not only the abstract.
2. Inspect tables/figures and external materials named in the paper when relevant.
3. If a method/detail cannot be established from the retrieved material, write `UNCLEAR / NOT REPORTED`; do not invent it.
4. Distinguish what the authors report from your independent appraisal.
5. Every interpretation of a quantitative result must preserve direction, magnitude and uncertainty when available.
6. Do not use observed/post hoc power as evidence that a completed result is true, false, absent or adequately precise.
7. Reporting checklists can locate information but cannot score validity.

# A — Paper anatomy and extraction discipline — 15 points

## A1. Build the evidence map — 6 points

For an unfamiliar randomized exercise/nutrition paper, list the order in which you would inspect at least **eight** of the following and state the purpose of each:

- title;
- abstract;
- introduction;
- methods;
- participant flow;
- results tables/figures;

A trial states:

Construct a compact table containing:

- Intervention;
- Comparator;
- Outcome;

- estimand/contrast;
- analysis population information still needed;
- one target-context field needed for applicability.

## A3. Result-table extraction — 4 points

The paper reports:

Record the result using the F0.9 result-table fields and write **one sentence** that is supported without treating `p=0.18` as proof of no effect.

# B — Prespecification, bias and inferential architecture — 20 points

## B1. Timeline audit — 7 points

Study timeline:

- recruitment starts: January 2023;
- final outcome collected: December 2023;
- registry first posted: February 2024;
- SAP uploaded: March 2024;
- manuscript submitted: July 2024.

The article calls the registered outcome “prespecified.”

Answer:

1. What can the registry/SAP make transparent?
2. What can they **not** establish from this timeline?
3. What wording should replace “prospectively prespecified” unless earlier evidence exists?
4. Why does retrospective registration not make the study automatically invalid?

## B2. Result-specific bias reasoning — 7 points

A randomized trial has:

- adequate allocation concealment;
- objective automated sprint time;
- subjective recovery rating assessed by an unblinded researcher;
- 4% missing sprint data, balanced across groups;
- 25% versus 8% missing recovery ratings, with dropout related to adverse symptoms;
- multiple eligible recovery scoring methods.

For **sprint time** and **recovery rating separately**:

- identify the most relevant bias mechanisms;
- state why a single “study quality score” is inadequate;
- state what is unknown if selection among recovery scoring methods was not prospectively documented.

## B3. Reporting versus validity — 3 points

Repair this statement:

> “The authors completed every CONSORT item, so the trial is low risk of bias.”

## B4. Design label versus appraisal — 3 points

Repair both:

- “It is randomized, therefore confounding and bias are solved.”
- “It is observational, therefore it provides no useful evidence.”

# C — Quantitative interpretation, multiplicity and robustness — 20 points

## C1. Within-group versus between-group — 5 points

A two-group trial reports:

- intervention: baseline-to-week-12 change `+8`, p<0.001;
- control: baseline-to-week-12 change `+5`, p=0.01;
- between-group difference in change `+3 [−1,+7]`.

What is the treatment-effect result? Interpret it using F0.5 logic.

## C2. “No difference” versus equivalence — 5 points

Authors write:

> “There was no significant group×time interaction (p=0.31), demonstrating equivalent adaptations.”

Explain why this conclusion does not follow automatically. State what additional quantitative/design information would be needed to support a formal equivalence or a defensible “no important difference” claim.

## C3. Observed/post hoc power — 4 points

A completed paper with `p=0.22` reports observed power of 28% and says:

> “The nonsignificant result was probably a false negative because power was low.”

Repair the interpretation. What should be examined instead?

## C4. Multiplicity and exploration — 6 points

A trial measures:

- one registered primary outcome;
- six secondary outcomes;
- three timepoints;
- four subgroups;
- two alternative covariate models.

After seeing the data, the authors highlight one subgroup×treatment p=0.03 that was not in the registry.

Explain:

- why counting “one p=0.03” is incomplete;
- how you would determine whether it is confirmatory or exploratory;
- what direct comparison is needed for subgroup inference;
- what a robust report should disclose.

- `1-RM` = maximum load completed for one repetition under the test protocol.

# Critical-fail conditions

A score >=80 does **not** pass F0.9 if the response commits any material structural failure below:

1. appraises the performance paper from abstract only;
2. uses `randomized` as a complete validity judgment;
3. substitutes CONSORT/checklist completion for appraisal;
4. states `p<0.05 = true/important`;
5. states `p>0.05 = no effect/equivalence`;
6. uses observed/post hoc power as posterior evidence or as rescue for the completed result;
7. ignores retrospective-versus-prospective prespecification when interpreting the performance paper;
8. ignores material multiplicity/selection opportunities;
9. treats reporting completeness as low risk of bias;
10. replaces result-specific RoB reasoning with one paper-level score/label;
11. confuses within-group time improvement with evidence that one treatment outperformed another;
12. extrapolates an acute/mechanistic outcome to chronic performance/body composition without evidence;
13. simply rewrites the authors' conclusion rather than reconstructing the estimand/results;
14. invents missing protocol/SAP/blinding/CI information;
15. treats one paper as a GRADE certainty rating for the entire evidence body.

---

# Scoring principle

Full credit requires **causal/statistical explanation**, not just labels. A response that merely fills boxes with yes/no/checkmarks cannot earn more than 60/100 even if many boxes are factually correct.
