## 2. Registered sources

### F0-S01 — CONSORT 2025 statement

**Citation:** Hopewell S, Chan A-W, Collins GS, et al. CONSORT 2025 statement: updated guideline for reporting randomised trials. *BMJ*. 2025;389:e081123. doi:10.1136/bmj-2024-081123.

**URL/DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-081123

**Source type:** international reporting guideline / consensus methods statement.

**Question answered:** What information should a contemporary randomized-trial report expose so readers can understand design, conduct, analysis, results and open-science materials?

**Population/context:** randomized trials; primarily common two-group parallel trials, with broader applicability and extensions.

**Key contribution:** current CONSORT revision; 30-item checklist; explicitly includes trial registration, accessible protocol/statistical analysis plan, data/code sharing, funding/conflicts, harms and transparent reporting of analyses. It supersedes CONSORT 2010.

**Key limitations:** reporting guidance, not a study-quality score and not a substitute for causal/risk-of-bias appraisal.

**Claims supported/contested:** transparent reporting is necessary for appraisal; prespecification and access to protocol/SAP are relevant to detecting undeclared analytical changes; complete reporting does not itself prove valid design/conduct.

**Modules:** F0.1, F0.2, F0.6, F0.9.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S02 — STROBE statement

**Citation:** von Elm E, Altman DG, Egger M, Pocock SJ, Gøtzsche PC, Vandenbroucke JP; STROBE Initiative. The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement: guidelines for reporting observational studies. *J Clin Epidemiol*. 2008;61(4):344-349. doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2007.11.008.

**URL:** https://www.strobe-statement.org/

**Source type:** reporting guideline.

**Question answered:** What should be transparently reported for cohort, case-control and cross-sectional studies?

**Population/context:** observational epidemiology.

**Key contribution:** structured reporting expectations for the principal analytical observational designs.

**Key limitations:** STROBE explicitly states that its checklist is guidance for reporting, not an instrument for evaluating methodological quality.

**Claims supported/contested:** observational design/reporting elements can be systematically located; checklist completion cannot be converted into causal certainty or a quality score.

**Modules:** F0.2, F0.8, F0.9.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S03 — PRISMA 2020

**Citation:** Page MJ, McKenzie JE, Bossuyt PM, et al. The PRISMA 2020 statement: an updated guideline for reporting systematic reviews. *BMJ*. 2021;372:n71. doi:10.1136/bmj.n71.

**URL:** https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020

**Source type:** systematic-review reporting guideline.

**Question answered:** What should be reported for a systematic review so methods, selection and synthesis can be understood and appraised?

**Population/context:** systematic reviews, especially reviews of intervention effects.

**Key contribution:** 27-item reporting framework, expanded checklist and flow diagrams for original/updated reviews.

**Key limitations:** PRISMA improves reporting transparency; it is not a risk-of-bias tool for included studies and is not itself proof that a review was well designed or executed.

**Claims supported/contested:** systematic-review appraisal requires visibility into search, eligibility, study flow and synthesis; reporting quality and evidence certainty are separate constructs.

**Modules:** F0.2, F0.7, F0.8, F0.9.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S04 — Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 10: Meta-analysis

**Citation:** Deeks JJ, Higgins JPT, Altman DG, et al. Chapter 10: Analysing data and undertaking meta-analyses. In: Higgins JPT, Thomas J, Chandler J, et al, eds. *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions*. Version 6.5. Cochrane; 2024.

**URL:** https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-10

**Source type:** methods handbook chapter.

**Question answered:** How should effect estimates be synthesized and heterogeneity handled/interpreted in intervention reviews?

**Population/context:** systematic reviews/meta-analyses of intervention studies.

**Key contribution:** effect synthesis, weighting, fixed/random-effects concepts, heterogeneity, subgroup/sensitivity methods and interpretation cautions.

**Key limitations:** technically dense; not a substitute for learning the underlying effect measures and study-level validity first.

**Claims supported/contested:** pooled estimates depend on defensible effect measures and assumptions; heterogeneity needs clinical/methodological as well as statistical interpretation.

**Modules:** F0.7.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S05 — Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, v6.5

**Citation:** Higgins JPT, Thomas J, Chandler J, Cumpston M, Li T, Page MJ, Welch VA, eds. *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions*. Version 6.5 (updated August 2024). Cochrane; 2024.

**URL:** https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current

**Source type:** authoritative methods handbook.

**Question answered:** What methodological framework underpins question formulation, study inclusion, risk of bias, synthesis and interpretation in systematic reviews of interventions?

**Population/context:** health-intervention evidence synthesis.

**Key contribution:** integrated current Cochrane methods; useful F0 chapters include question formulation, effect measures, bias/conflicts, RoB 2, synthesis/meta-analysis and certainty/interpretation.

**Key limitations:** designed for systematic reviews rather than a general introductory statistics course; individual chapters may have different last-update dates within v6.5.

**Claims supported/contested:** methodological hierarchy must be question-specific; bias, synthesis and applicability require explicit reasoning rather than mechanical study-design ranking.

**Modules:** F0.1, F0.7, F0.8.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S06 — Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 6: Effect measures

**Citation:** Higgins JPT, Li T, Deeks JJ, et al. Chapter 6: Choosing effect measures and computing estimates of effect. In: Higgins JPT, Thomas J, Chandler J, et al, eds. *Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions*. Version 6.5. Cochrane; 2024.

**URL:** https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-06

**Source type:** methods handbook chapter.

**Question answered:** How are common intervention effect measures defined, selected and interpreted for continuous, dichotomous and other outcomes?

**Population/context:** intervention studies and evidence synthesis.

**Key contribution:** anchors raw/standardized continuous effects and relative/absolute dichotomous effects to the outcome scale and synthesis problem.

**Key limitations:** not intended as a first exposure to arithmetic; must be taught with worked examples and practical thresholds.

**Claims supported/contested:** effect measure choice changes interpretation; absolute and relative effects answer different practical questions.

**Modules:** F0.5, F0.7.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

**URL:** https://www.riskofbias.info/

**Source type:** official operational hub for domain-based risk-of-bias tools.

**Question answered:** Which structured tools are used for risk-of-bias assessment in randomized trials and non-randomized studies of interventions/exposures?

**Key contribution:** access point for RoB 2, ROBINS-I, ROBINS-E and ROB ME.

**Claims supported/contested:** risk of bias is domain- and result-specific; randomized and non-randomized studies require different causal/bias considerations.

**Modules:** F0.3, F0.8, F0.9.

### F0-S08 — ASA Statement on p-values

**Citation:** Wasserstein RL, Lazar NA. The ASA Statement on p-Values: Context, Process, and Purpose. *The American Statistician*. 2016;70(2):129-133. doi:10.1080/00031305.2016.1154108.

**Official ASA resource:** https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf

**Source type:** professional statistical association statement.

**Question answered:** What can and cannot be concluded from a p-value?

**Population/context:** quantitative scientific inference broadly.

**Key contribution:** six core principles: p-values address incompatibility under a specified statistical model; they do not measure probability a hypothesis is true, effect magnitude/importance or evidential worth in isolation; selective reporting invalidates interpretation.

**Key limitations:** does not prescribe one universal replacement inferential framework.

**Claims supported/contested:** `p < 0.05` is not a truth threshold; scientific conclusions require design, effect magnitude, uncertainty, transparency and context.

**Modules:** F0.5, F0.6, F0.10.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S09 — ASA Task Force Statement on Statistical Significance and Replicability

**Citation:** Benjamini Y, et al. ASA President’s Task Force Statement on Statistical Significance and Replicability. American Statistical Association / IMS; 2021.

**URL:** https://magazine.amstat.org/blog/2021/08/01/task-force-statement-p-value/

**Source type:** professional-statistics task-force statement.

**Question answered:** How should p-values/significance tests be placed within broader uncertainty and replicability reasoning after the debate over abandoning “statistical significance”?

**Population/context:** scientific inference broadly.

**Key contribution:** clarifies that p-values and significance tests can be useful when properly applied; emphasizes uncertainty, variability, multiplicity, replicability and context rather than simplistic thresholding.

**Key limitations:** high-level principles rather than a technical training manual.

**Claims supported/contested:** neither ritual thresholding nor wholesale abandonment of statistical methods is adequate; multiple uncertainty measures and explicit decision consequences matter.

**Modules:** F0.5, F0.6, F0.10.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S10 — FDA Multiple Endpoints Guidance

**Citation:** U.S. Food and Drug Administration. *Multiple Endpoints in Clinical Trials: Guidance for Industry*. Final Guidance. October 2022.

**URL:** https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/multiple-endpoints-clinical-trials

**Source type:** regulatory statistical guidance.

**Question answered:** Why do multiple endpoints/hypotheses increase false-positive risk and how can multiplicity be prospectively managed?

**Population/context:** confirmatory drug/biologic clinical trials; principles are pedagogically useful beyond regulation.

**Key contribution:** explicit treatment of endpoint multiplicity, grouping/order and recognized methods to control erroneous conclusions.

**Key limitations:** regulatory scope; sports-science exploratory studies may have different decision contexts, but the mathematical multiplicity problem remains.

**Claims supported/contested:** unaddressed multiplicity can produce false/misleading claims; endpoint hierarchy and prespecified analysis matter.

**Modules:** F0.6.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S11 — Catalogue of Bias

**Citation:** Catalogue of Bias Collaboration / Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine, University of Oxford. *Catalogue of Bias*.

**URL:** https://catalogofbias.org/biases/

**Source type:** curated educational/methodological bias catalogue.

**Question answered:** What named biases can distort design, conduct, analysis, interpretation and dissemination, and through what mechanisms?

**Population/context:** clinical/health research, with broad methodological transfer.

**Key contribution:** definitions, examples, impacts and prevention/mitigation context for many biases; useful for concrete counterexamples.

**Key limitations:** a catalogue can encourage name memorization; F0 must teach causal mechanism of distortion rather than “spot the bias name.”

**Claims supported/contested:** biases can alter magnitude/direction at multiple research stages; identifying a label is insufficient without explaining the distortion pathway.

**Modules:** F0.3, F0.8.

### F0-S12 — NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods

**Citation:** NIST/SEMATECH. *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*. National Institute of Standards and Technology.

**URL:** https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/

**Source type:** institutional statistical reference.

**Question answered:** How are core descriptive distributions, hypothesis tests and confidence intervals defined and related in frequentist practice?

**Population/context:** general applied statistics; examples are often engineering/process-oriented.

**Key contribution:** stable reference for distributions, descriptive methods, hypothesis testing and confidence-interval mechanics/interpretation.

**Key limitations:** context is not sports/biomedicine and some presentation is traditional; F0 must pair it with ASA guidance and domain examples to avoid binary testing habits.

**Claims supported/contested:** confidence intervals quantify sampling uncertainty under assumptions; hypothesis tests and confidence intervals are mathematically related but answer should not collapse to a yes/no label.

**Modules:** F0.4, F0.5.

### F0-S13 — Sample Size Justification

**Citation:** Lakens D. Sample Size Justification. *Collabra: Psychology*. 2022;8(1):33267. doi:10.1525/collabra.33267.

**URL/DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267

**Source type:** peer-reviewed methodological review/tutorial.

**Question answered:** How can sample size be justified in relation to inferential goals rather than by rote convention?

**Population/context:** quantitative empirical research broadly.

**Key contribution:** distinguishes census/resource constraints, a-priori power, precision, heuristics and explicit lack of justification; links informativeness to smallest effects of interest, precision and error rates.

**Key limitations:** not a consensus guideline; examples and emphasis arise largely from behavioral-science methodology, so principles must be translated carefully to exercise/nutrition designs.

**Claims supported/contested:** power is conditional on assumptions and target effects; sample size should be justified by the information needed, not by a magical universal N.

**Modules:** F0.5, F0.6.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S14 — ICH E9 / E9(R1)

**Citation:** International Council for Harmonisation. *ICH E9: Statistical Principles for Clinical Trials*; and *ICH E9(R1) Addendum on Estimands and Sensitivity Analysis in Clinical Trials*.

**Official access:** https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/ich-e9-statistical-principles-clinical-trials-scientific-guideline

**Source type:** international regulatory methodological guideline.

**Question answered:** How should trial objectives, design, analysis, estimands and sensitivity analyses align so the treatment effect being estimated is clear?

**Population/context:** clinical trials for medicinal products.

**Key contribution:** rigorous vocabulary for objectives, statistical principles and the estimand framework; useful for teaching that the question, treatment condition, population, endpoint/intercurrent events and summary measure must align.

**Key limitations:** regulatory drug-trial scope and technicality exceed what F0 initially needs; use conceptually rather than importing all regulatory detail.

**Claims supported/contested:** ambiguous treatment-effect questions create ambiguous analyses; sensitivity analysis explores robustness relative to assumptions and the estimand.

**Modules:** F0.1, F0.2, F0.4, F0.5.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S16 — Causal Inference: What If

**Citation:** Hernán MA, Robins JM. *Causal Inference: What If*. Boca Raton: Chapman & Hall/CRC; 2020. Free online edition maintained by the authors.

**URL:** https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook

**Source type:** advanced causal-inference textbook, freely available from authors.

**Question answered:** How should causal effects be defined and identified using counterfactual reasoning, randomized experiments and observational data?

**Population/context:** epidemiology and causal inference broadly.

### F0-S17 — Target-trial framework

**Citation:** Hernán MA, Dahabreh IJ, Dickerman BA, Swanson SA. The Target Trial Framework for Causal Inference From Observational Data: Why and When Is It Helpful? *Ann Intern Med*. 2025;178(3):402-407. doi:10.7326/ANNALS-24-01871.

**URL/DOI:** https://doi.org/10.7326/ANNALS-24-01871

**Source type:** peer-reviewed causal-methods article.

**Question answered:** How can specifying a hypothetical randomized target trial clarify causal questions and reduce avoidable design bias in observational analyses?

**Population/context:** observational causal inference about intervention effects.

**Key contribution:** practical two-step frame: specify the target-trial protocol, then emulate it with observational data; clarifies that design emulation can reduce some biases but cannot repair inadequate data.

**Key limitations:** not every observational question is an intervention-effect question; target-trial emulation does not create randomization or eliminate unmeasured confounding.

**Claims supported/contested:** clearer design can prevent self-inflicted bias; causal validity still depends on data and assumptions.

**Modules:** F0.3.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S19 — GRADE Book: principles for assessing certainty of interventions

**Citation:** Goldkuhle M, Kreuzberger N, Neumann I, et al. Principles for assessing the certainty of interventions. In: *The GRADE Book*. GRADE Working Group. Last modified 21 August 2025.

**URL:** https://book.gradepro.org/guideline/principles-for-assessing-the-certainty-of-interventions

**Source type:** current official living GRADE guidance.

**Question answered:** How should certainty in intervention-effect estimates be conceptualized and assessed across a body of evidence?

**Population/context:** intervention evidence bodies.

**Key contribution:** certainty tied to ranges/decision thresholds, outcome-level evidence bodies and transparent independent judgments; cautions against double counting overlapping certainty domains.

**Key limitations:** requires prior understanding of effect estimates, bias and synthesis; should not be introduced as a memorized downgrade checklist.

**Claims supported/contested:** certainty describes confidence in where the true effect lies relative to a range/threshold, not confidence that a paper is “good.”

**Modules:** F0.8, F0.9, F0.10.

**Last checked:** 2026-09-08.

### F0-S20 — GRADE Book: indirectness

**Citation:** Schünemann HJ, Neumann I, Brennan S, et al. Indirectness. In: *The GRADE Book*. GRADE Working Group. Last modified 12 May 2026.

**URL:** https://book.gradepro.org/guideline/indirectness

**Source type:** current official living GRADE guidance.

**Question answered:** How should applicability/generalizability/transferability concerns be judged when evidence PICO differs from the target question?

**Population/context:** evidence synthesis and decision-making.

**Key contribution:** explicitly connects indirectness to mismatches in population, intervention, comparator and outcome, plus indirect comparisons.

**Key limitations:** applicability also requires domain knowledge and context not fully captured by a checklist.

**Claims supported/contested:** external applicability is not binary; differences between evidence and target context can lower confidence in the inference being transferred.

**Modules:** F0.8, F0.10.

## 3. Research notes and version controls

- `CONSORT 2025` is the current general CONSORT statement and explicitly supersedes CONSORT 2010.
- The current Cochrane Handbook located during this research is version 6.5, updated August 2024.
- GRADE is actively transitioning from the older handbook to the living `GRADE Book`; the official GRADE site states that the Book is the most current resource and is intended to replace the previous Handbook by the end of 2026. For a chapter not yet replaced/complete, consult the official GRADE living map/legacy guidance rather than silently assuming the old handbook is current.
- STROBE remains useful for observational reporting, but its own documentation explicitly warns that the checklist is not a methodological-quality instrument.
- Methodological sources above are the F0 backbone. Later lesson authoring should add carefully selected open-access nutrition/exercise exemplar papers to teach transfer, but those exemplars must not be mistaken for general methodological authorities.
