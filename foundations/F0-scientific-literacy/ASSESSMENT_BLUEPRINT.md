# F0 — ASSESSMENT BLUEPRINT

**State:** `ARCHITECTED`
**Assessment purpose:** determine whether the learner can independently interpret and criticize scientific evidence at the level required before domain-specific sports-nutrition claims are treated as trustworthy.

## 1. What the exit assessment certifies

Passing the F0 exit assessment means the learner has demonstrated an integrated methodological baseline across F0.1–F0.10.

It does **not** automatically set every F0 topic to `MASTERED`. A first strong performance can support `APPLIED`/`INTEGRATED` status. `MASTERED` still requires robust performance over time, including later cumulative retesting under `MASTERY_PROTOCOL.md`.

## 2. Assessment format

Use a mixed-format assessment with five sections. Total: **100 points**.

| Section | Weight | Main competencies |
|---|---:|---|
| A. Question, design and causal structure | 20 | F0.1–F0.3 |
| B. Quantitative interpretation | 25 | F0.4–F0.6 |
| C. Systematic review and certainty | 20 | F0.7–F0.8 |
| D. Full-paper critical appraisal | 25 | F0.1–F0.9 integrated |
| E. Communication of uncertainty | 10 | F0.10 integrated |

The assessment must include at least one complete open-access human exercise/nutrition paper and one evidence-synthesis extract/forest plot. Exact substantive papers are selected when the assessment packet is authored, not in this architecture step.

## 3. Passing gate

A learner passes the F0 exit gate only if all conditions are met:

1. **total score ≥ 85/100**;
2. **each section ≥ 70% of its available points**;
3. no `CRITICAL_FAIL` condition occurs;
4. full-paper appraisal demonstrates a defensible chain from question → design → bias → estimate → uncertainty → applicability → conclusion;
5. uncertainty communication does not materially overstate what the evidence establishes.

Scores of 75–84 with no critical fail indicate `NEAR_PASS / TARGETED_REMEDIATION`, not failure of the whole block. Scores below 75 require broader remediation mapped to the earliest broken prerequisites.

## 4. Critical-fail conditions

Any of the following blocks the exit regardless of total points:

- claiming that an observational association is causal solely because it is statistically significant or covariate-adjusted;
- defining `p > 0.05` as proof that there is no effect when meaningful effects remain compatible with the interval/data;
- interpreting a 95% confidence interval as a 95% posterior probability that the fixed true parameter lies inside the observed interval;
- treating statistical significance as synonymous with practical importance;
- treating CONSORT, STROBE or PRISMA checklist completion as proof of low risk of bias/high study quality;
- treating a low I² value as proof that studies are clinically/methodologically identical;
- ignoring obvious multiplicity while making a confirmatory claim from one favorable result among many analyses;
- converting a low/very-low certainty evidence body into a definitive recommendation without explicit justification;
- failing to distinguish “absence of evidence” from evidence sufficiently precise to exclude a meaningful effect;
- fabricating information not present in the paper, supplement or assessment materials.

Critical fails are conceptual safety gates because these errors would corrupt later interpretation of nutrition evidence even if many definitions were memorized correctly.

## 5. Section A — Question, design and causal structure (20 points)

### A1. Operationalize a claim — 5 points

Given a vague claim such as “supplement X improves performance,” the learner must specify:

- target population;
- intervention/exposure;
- comparator;
- outcome and measurement window;
- effect/contrast of interest;
- one plausible result that would count against the claim.

**Rubric**

- 5: precise, internally coherent and empirically answerable;
- 3–4: mostly correct with one material ambiguity;
- 1–2: recognizes some PICO elements but claim remains operationally vague;
- 0: merely restates the slogan.

### A2. Design identification and inference boundary — 7 points

Present 3–4 short study descriptions spanning at least:

- randomized parallel or crossover trial;
- prospective cohort;
- cross-sectional or retrospective analysis.

For each, ask:

1. identify the design;
2. state its strongest defensible inference;
3. state one inference the design cannot establish by itself.

### A3. Causal diagram / bias mechanism — 8 points

Provide a simple exposure–outcome scenario with at least four covariates. Learner must classify plausible confounder/mediator/collider roles at conceptual level, identify an adjustment choice that could introduce bias, and explain the pathway in words.

This tests reasoning, not DAG software proficiency.

## 6. Section B — Quantitative interpretation (25 points)

### B1. Descriptive statistics and graph reading — 5 points

Tasks must include:

- selecting mean/SD versus median/IQR for a given distribution;
- distinguishing SD from SE;
- reading a graph with unequal axes or another misleading visual choice;
- interpreting absolute versus relative change.

### B2. Effect estimate + CI + practical threshold — 8 points

Give a result containing a point estimate, 95% CI and prespecified smallest effect of practical interest.

Learner must state:

- direction;
- magnitude;
- precision;
- whether data are compatible with meaningful benefit, trivial effect and/or harm;
- what cannot be inferred from statistical significance alone.

Full credit requires threshold-aware reasoning rather than checking whether the interval crosses a null value only.

### B3. p-value interpretation — 4 points

Use at least two statements where the learner must mark true/false and repair the false statement, e.g.:

- “p=0.03 means there is a 3% chance the null hypothesis is true.”
- “p=0.12 proves there is no meaningful effect.”

No credit for marking false without a correct replacement interpretation.

### B4. Power, precision and sample size — 4 points

Provide two studies with different sample sizes/interval widths. Ask which is more informative for a defined smallest effect of interest and why.

Required distinction: power is design/assumption dependent; precision is visible in estimate uncertainty; non-significance does not diagnose adequate power.

### B5. Multiplicity and analytical flexibility — 4 points

Provide a trial/report with multiple outcomes, time points or subgroup analyses. Learner must identify:

- the family of analyses creating multiplicity;
- whether a highlighted result is confirmatory or exploratory from the information given;
- what registration/protocol/SAP information would be needed to judge prespecification.

## 7. Section C — Systematic review and certainty (20 points)

### C1. Forest plot interpretation — 8 points

Use a forest plot with at least five studies and visible heterogeneity.

Learner must identify:

- effect direction and scale;
- study weights at conceptual level;
- pooled estimate and uncertainty;
- heterogeneity signal;
- at least two plausible clinical/methodological sources of heterogeneity;
- why I² alone does not determine whether pooling is valid.

### C2. Review-method appraisal — 5 points

Provide a short methods extract from a systematic review. Ask the learner to identify two features that affect trustworthiness, such as:

- eligibility definition;
- search breadth;
- duplicate screening/extraction;
- risk-of-bias assessment;
- outcome selection;
- model/sensitivity analyses.

The learner must distinguish missing reporting from demonstrated methodological failure when the text does not resolve the issue.

### C3. Certainty and applicability — 7 points

Provide two evidence-body summaries with the same approximate point estimate but different properties (e.g., one precise/direct/low risk of bias; another imprecise/indirect/high risk of bias).

Learner must:

- assign a relative certainty judgment with justification;
- identify which GRADE-style domains drive the difference;
- explain whether the evidence applies to a specified athlete population/context;
- avoid treating recommendation strength as identical to certainty of one outcome.

## 8. Section D — Full-paper critical appraisal (25 points)

### Paper selection criteria

The assessment paper must:

- involve humans and an exercise/nutrition-relevant exposure or intervention;
- be legally accessible to the learner in full text;
- contain enough methodological detail for meaningful appraisal;
- include quantitative results with uncertainty;
- not require domain knowledge beyond what can be supplied in a short neutral context box;
- preferably include protocol/registration/supplement links so prespecification can be assessed;
- contain at least one nontrivial appraisal issue but not be a deliberately fraudulent or absurd paper.

Rotate papers between assessment versions to reduce answer memorization.

### Appraisal template and scoring

| Domain | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Question/estimand | 3 | reconstructs target contrast and outcome/time frame |
| Design/selection | 3 | identifies design and sampling/allocation implications |
| Measurement/intervention fidelity | 2 | recognizes outcome/exposure/intervention measurement limitations |
| Bias/confounding/missingness | 4 | prioritizes material threats and explains mechanisms |
| Analysis/prespecification/multiplicity | 3 | checks analysis plan, multiplicity and exploratory status |
| Effect estimate/uncertainty | 4 | interprets magnitude, interval and practical importance correctly |
| Applicability | 2 | compares study context with target population/context |
| Authors’ conclusion | 2 | judges whether language is proportional to results/design |
| What the study does not prove | 2 | explicitly bounds inference |

A response that only fills a checklist with yes/no answers cannot earn more than 60% of this section.

## 9. Section E — Communication of uncertainty (10 points)

Give the learner the same evidence result and ask for two outputs:

1. **technical synthesis** — maximum ~150 words;
2. **plain-language synthesis** — maximum ~100 words.

Both must preserve:

- population/context;
- effect direction/magnitude;
- uncertainty/precision;
- certainty/major limitation;
- what is not established;
- practical relevance when a threshold is available.

### Scoring

- epistemic calibration: 4 points;
- preservation of magnitude/uncertainty: 2 points;
- scope/applicability: 2 points;
- clarity without distortion: 2 points.

## 11. Remediation mapping

After scoring, do not simply repeat the whole block. Map errors to the earliest broken dependency.

| Error pattern | Primary remediation |
|---|---|
| vague/unanswerable claim framing | F0.1 |
| design hierarchy memorized without inference boundaries | F0.2 |
| adjusted association assumed causal | F0.3 → F0.2 |
| SD/SE/distribution confusion | F0.4 / quantitative bridge |
| CI/p-value/practical-significance error | F0.5 → F0.4 if needed |
| power/multiplicity misunderstanding | F0.6 → F0.5 |
| pooled estimate/I² overinterpretation | F0.7 → F0.5/F0.6 as needed |
| reporting checklist used as quality score | F0.8 → F0.2/F0.3 |
| full-paper critique becomes summary only | F0.9 with targeted prior-unit repair |
| overconfident language | F0.10 → F0.8/F0.5 depending cause |

All persistent errors must be recorded in `study/ERROR_LEDGER.md` when actual learner testing begins.

## 12. Retest design

A remediation retest must use new numbers, new scenarios and—where relevant—a different paper. Repeating the same item is recall of the answer, not evidence of transfer.

Recommended validation sequence after a first pass:

1. immediate exit assessment;
2. targeted remediation if needed;
3. alternate-form retest;
4. delayed cumulative retrieval during F1/F2;
5. later evidence-appraisal task in a domain module;
6. only then consider `MASTERED` if performance remains robust.

## 13. Assessment QA before release

Before any concrete F0 exam is used, verify:

- every scored item maps to a declared F0 competency;
- calculations have a verified answer key;
- no question depends on hidden domain knowledge;
- article/supplement links work;
- reporting-guideline items are not scored as proxy quality metrics;
- effect sizes/intervals are interpreted in context;
- uncertainty admits nuance where more than one defensible conclusion is possible;
- critical-fail items truly represent structural misconceptions;
- answer key explains reasoning, not just final labels;
- public-repository copyright rules are respected.

## 14. Exit-state rule

Passing F0 permits progression because the methodological gateway has been demonstrated. It does not imply professional credentialing, clinical authority or completion of sports-nutrition mastery.
